# Example Authority — Guide to advertising health supplements (FICTIONAL, for development only)

Version 2, 2026. This fictional guide is written for testing; it is not any real regulator's text.

## 1. Scope
1.1 This guide applies to health supplements and food supplements sold in the market, including vitamins, minerals,
herbal and gummy supplements, in any advertising, including live-stream selling.

## 2. Prohibited claims
2.1 An advertisement must not claim or imply that a supplement prevents, treats, cures or relieves any disease,
for example colds, flu, diabetes, arthritis or cancer.
2.2 An advertisement must not compare a supplement with a medicine or claim it replaces prescribed medication.
2.3 An advertisement must not claim that a supplement is free of side effects or safe for everyone.
2.4 An advertisement must not promise weight loss of a stated amount or within a stated time.

## 3. Claims that need care
3.1 Function claims (for example "vitamin C contributes to the normal function of the immune system") are acceptable
only in the approved wording and must not be overstated ("boosts immunity", "never get sick").
3.2 Testimonials must not be used to make claims the advertiser itself may not make.
3.3 Statements to pregnant or breastfeeding women and to children must advise consulting a doctor and must not
promise safety.
